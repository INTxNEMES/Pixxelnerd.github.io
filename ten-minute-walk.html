The ten-minute walk that made me a better designer

13 Nov 2026

Some of my best design ideas didn’t come from a screen. They came from walking around with a camera and no plan.

When I got stuck on a layout, every version looked the same. So I closed the laptop and went outside for ten minutes with my phone. I wasn’t looking for inspiration. I just wanted to look at things properly for once.

Notice what repeats

Within a few minutes I saw rhythm everywhere: the evenly spaced bars of a railing, windows lined up on a wall, tree shadows falling across a road. Designers talk about repetition and spacing all the time, but it’s different when you see it in a real place. Once you notice it, you start to feel when a layout has it and when it’s missing.

Look for the one thing that stands out

I kept photographing the same kind of moment: one warm, bright thing among dull surroundings, like a red door on a grey street. Your eye goes straight to it. That’s hierarchy, and nobody had to explain it. A good design usually has one clear thing you see first. When everything shouts, nothing gets heard.

Pay attention to empty space

Beginners tend to fill every corner of a frame or a page. But the photos I liked most had room in them: a lone figure on a wide road, a small bird against a big sky. The empty space made the subject feel important. Whitespace does the same job in design. It isn’t wasted, it lets the content breathe.

Light does the real work

The same scene looks completely different at 8 a.m. and at noon. Soft morning light feels calm, and harsh midday light adds drama. Designers set mood through colour and contrast in the same way, but we often forget the feeling comes from the light, not from the objects.

Try it yourself

You don’t need a good camera. Next time you’re stuck:

  1. Walk for ten minutes with no destination.
  2. Photograph only patterns, contrast or empty space.
  3. Look through the photos afterwards and ask what made each one work.

Then take that answer back to your design. I came home with a dozen mediocre photos and one solved layout. The camera didn’t teach me anything new, but it made me look at what was already there.
